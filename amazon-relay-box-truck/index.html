Amazon Relay for Box Trucks

Yes, you can run Amazon Relay with a box truck. Amazon accepts 16 ft and 26 ft box trucks with a working tuck-under liftgate. Cargo vans are not supported. Here's how to get started and what to expect.

Box Truck Equipment Specs

Amazon's box truck requirements are precise:

  • 16 ft box truck with a working tuck-under liftgate
  • 26 ft box truck with a working tuck-under liftgate
  • The liftgate is required—Amazon will not accept a box truck without one

Cargo vans are not supported. This includes sprinter vans, Ford Transit vans, and any vehicle registered as a van. Even if you think a cargo van could handle a load, Amazon's system will not allow you to book on Relay with one.

The liftgate is mandatory because Amazon's loads are palletized freight. The liftgate lets you load and unload pallets quickly at a dock or ground level without manual labor. Without a liftgate, you'd have to hand-unload freight, which is time-consuming and violates Amazon's loading standards.

Box Truck Load Availability

Box truck loads post regularly on the Amazon Relay board. You'll see:

  • Local / Regional Runs: Short-haul deliveries to nearby distribution centers or retail sites, picked up early morning and dropped same day.
  • Cross-Dock Work: Pick up from one Amazon facility and drop at another. These are frequent and typically predictable in timing.
  • Third-Party LTL: Less-than-truckload freight that Amazon brokers or Amazon-vetted shippers post. These often come from small vendors or retailers using Amazon's logistics network.
  • Seasonal Spikes: During peak seasons (Q4, Prime Day), box truck loads increase significantly.

Box truck loads do not get the premium freight that dedicated reefers or large trailers get. The best loads (high pay, convenient timing) usually go to tractor-trailer carriers. Box truck loads tend to be shorter haul, more frequent turnarounds, and lower per-mile rates.

But box truck carriers have advantages: lower fuel costs per mile, easier parking and dock access at smaller facilities, and lower insurance costs. For an owner-operator with a box truck, Relay offers steady work without constant hunting for loads.

Finding Box Truck Loads on the Board

When you log into the Amazon Relay load board, you'll filter by equipment type. Set your filter to "26 ft Box Truck" or "16 ft Box Truck" (whichever you own), and the board shows only loads that your equipment can haul.

Good loads disappear in seconds. This is why many box truck carriers use auto-refresh tools or auto-booking extensions:

  • Refresher Extension: Automatically reload the board every 1-5 seconds so you see new loads instantly.
  • Alert Mode: Get pinged on your phone when a load matching your filters appears.
  • Auto-Book Mode: Automatically book a load if it meets your price and lane rules.

Even with an extension, competition is fierce. A good rate at a convenient time can book within seconds. This is why automation tools are nearly essential for box truck carriers who want to maximize load volume.

Rate Variability for Box Trucks

Box truck rates on Amazon Relay vary based on several factors:

Season Peak seasons (Q4 holiday, Prime Day in summer) offer higher rates because demand is high and Amazon has more freight. Off-season rates tend to soften as demand drops.

Time of Day Early morning loads often pay better than afternoon or evening. Amazon plans most of its distribution work around early picks and mid-morning drops, so early slots fill first and command premium rates.

Origin and Destination Local, high-volume lanes pay better per load but lower per mile. Cross-country or remote drops (rural areas, small towns) pay more per mile but are less frequent. Box truck loads rarely go cross-country; most are regional.

Day of Week Monday-Wednesday usually see higher volumes and better rates than Thursday-Friday. Weekends (Saturday, Sunday) rarely have box truck loads.

Your Own Booking History If you accept most loads and have a high tender-acceptance rate, Amazon's algorithm may show you better-priced loads. If you decline frequently, Amazon deprioritizes your recommendations, and you see older, less attractive freight.

Volume on the Board That Day When many box trucks are online, rates soften because there's competition. When few are online (early morning, late evening, bad weather), rates can spike.

Amazon Relay does not publish "typical" box truck rates because they vary so much. Check the board yourself during your target hours and see what's posting. A rate that's good one day might be soft the next.

The Economics of Box Truck Work

To evaluate whether a Relay load makes sense, you need to calculate net profit:

Gross Rate (what Amazon pays) minus Fuel, Maintenance, Deadhead to Pickup = Net Profit.

On every load, calculate the posted rate minus your estimated fuel, maintenance and deadhead costs. The remainder is your profit for that run. Many carriers set a profit floor—the minimum net profit below which they won't take a load. This protects you from unprofitable work.

Amazon Relay auto-booker extensions like NudoIQ let you set a profit floor automatically. They calculate net profit on every load and only book if it meets your minimum. This removes the guesswork and prevents low-margin mistakes.

Advantages of Running a Box Truck on Relay

  • No Haggling: Rates are set by Amazon. You accept or decline; there's no negotiation.
  • Steady Work: Box truck loads post daily. You're rarely scrambling for freight.
  • Weekly Pay: Amazon pays every Friday for loads completed Sunday-Saturday. Reliable cash flow.
  • Predictable Hours: Box truck loads are typically day-length runs, so you can plan your week.
  • Lower Operating Cost: Fuel, insurance and maintenance are cheaper than a tractor.

Disadvantages and Challenges

  • Lower Per-Mile Rates: Box truck loads pay less per mile than reefers or dedicated flatbeds because the freight is lower-value or shorter-haul.
  • Board Competition: You're competing with other box truck carriers for the same loads. Speed and automation matter.
  • Limited Deadhead: If you're far from the pickup, the deadhead eats into profit. Box truck loads tend to be local, so deadhead is usually manageable.
  • Scorecard Pressure: Amazon's performance scorecard can be harsh. One missed pickup or late drop can tank your score and reduce load access.

Getting Started with a Box Truck on Relay

  1. Confirm Equipment Requirements: You have a 16 ft or 26 ft box truck with a working tuck-under liftgate.
  2. Meet Amazon's Eligibility Requirements: 180-day DOT authority, valid MC number, acceptable BASIC scores, insurance minimums.
  3. Sign Up at relay.amazon.com: Submit your application and insurance certificates.
  4. Add Your Box Truck to Your Profile: List the truck's specs, year, capacity and current location.
  5. Start Browsing the Load Board: Log in, filter by your equipment type, and refresh to see available loads.
  6. Consider an Auto-Refresh Tool: To keep up with competition, many carriers use an extension to refresh the board automatically and get alerts when new loads post.
  7. Set Your Profit Floor and Rules: If using an auto-booking extension, configure your minimum profit, lane preferences and max-books-per-day cap.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a DOT number to run a box truck on Amazon Relay?

Yes. Amazon requires a DOT number with interstate authority (active for at least 180 days), an MC number, and acceptable safety ratings and BASIC scores. You cannot apply as a gig driver or without formal carrier authority.

Can I use a sprinter van or Ford Transit?

No. Amazon does not accept cargo vans or small commercial vans. Only 16 ft and 26 ft box trucks with a tuck-under liftgate qualify.

What if my box truck doesn't have a liftgate?

You cannot book Relay loads without a liftgate. Amazon's freight is palletized and requires a lift to load/unload efficiently. Install one before applying, or it will limit you to hand-unload carriers (not Relay).

What's the typical pay for a box truck load?

It varies by lane, season and time. You must calculate net profit on each load individually—posted rate minus your fuel, maintenance and deadhead costs—to know if it's worth accepting.

How many box truck loads can I book in a day?

As many as the board offers and you want to accept. It depends on your setup and profit targets. NudoIQ lets you set a max-books cap so you don't over-commit.

Is it worth running Relay if I'm far from Amazon facilities?

Depends on your location. If you're near a major Amazon distribution center, loads will be available and deadhead costs will be reasonable. If you're very far from the nearest facility, deadhead costs might make most loads unprofitable.
