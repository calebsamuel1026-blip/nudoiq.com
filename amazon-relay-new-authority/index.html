Does Amazon Relay accept new authority?

New authority and want Amazon loads? Here's how Amazon words its 180-day rule, why it says to apply early, and what it says about leasing on.

NudoIQ · Published October 16, 2026 · Last updated October 16, 2026

Short answer

You can apply, but onboarding usually waits. Amazon says Relay typically requires your DOT number to be at least 180 days old to complete onboarding, and you need a valid MC number. It still encourages you to apply early.

Does Amazon Relay accept new authority?

You can apply. Onboarding usually waits for 180 days. The Relay FAQ says: "While Relay typically requires your DOT number to be at least 180 days old to complete onboarding, we encourage you to apply early."

Amazon's reason: "Starting your application now allows us to begin reviewing your information so you're ready to move forward as soon as you meet the requirement."

"Typically" is Amazon's word. It publishes no exception or new-authority program.

How does Amazon word the 180-day rule?

Four ways, on different pages:

Amazon pageWhat it says
Relay FAQ, requirements"A DOT number, with interstate authority and that has been active for minimum of 180 days"
Relay FAQ, new carriers"your DOT number to be at least 180 days old to complete onboarding"
Insurance post"your MC must be active for at least 180 days before you haul with Relay"
Box truck loads post"you'll need a DOT authority for at least 6 months"

Three name the DOT number or DOT authority, one names the MC, and one says 6 months instead of 180 days. Amazon doesn't say which date it counts from: your USDOT registration, your authority grant or the day it went active.

Check your dates on FMCSA's company lookup, and ask Amazon if they differ.

Do you need an MC number, or is DOT enough?

Both. Amazon lists a DOT number with interstate authority and "a valid MC number." Your entity also has to be a "Carrier" that is "Authorized for Property and for Hire."

More in our owner-operator guide and the full requirements list.

Can you lease on to another carrier first?

Leasing on is an FMCSA arrangement. Amazon says nothing about it for Relay.

What FMCSA's rules say:

  • A lease is a contract where the owner "grants the use of equipment, with or without driver, for a specified period to an authorized carrier" (49 CFR 376.2).
  • "There shall be a written lease granting the use of the equipment" (49 CFR 376.11).
  • The carrier you lease to "shall assume complete responsibility for the operation of the equipment for the duration of the lease" (49 CFR 376.12).

So a leased-on truck runs under the other carrier's authority.

On Relay, the only route Amazon describes under another carrier is the driver invite: "A carrier needs to invite their drivers to create a profile in the Relay app." See Amazon Relay for drivers.

Amazon doesn't say whether time leased on counts toward your own 180 days. Don't assume it does. Confirm the lease itself with FMCSA.

Should you rent or buy an aged MC?

We won't advise it. Amazon says:

  • "Never transfer or sell your account to someone you don't know." (email scams post)
  • It lists "Unsolicited offers to buy your business or manage your loads" as a scam sign.
  • Ownership changes go through Amazon: "email relay-ownership-transfers@amazon.com at least 30 days ahead."
  • Amazon verifies every owner with more than 25% ownership, and it screens "operating authority, ownership, affiliations, and other business credentials."

Amazon publishes nothing about renting an MC. Buying a company? See Amazon Relay business for sale. Account offers are covered in our fraud guide.

What can you do while your authority ages?

  1. Apply now. Amazon says it will "keep you updated on key program news while you wait."
  2. Match FMCSA exactly. Your application details should match the FMCSA database "down to the last dash."
  3. Line up insurance. The minimums are in our insurance guide.
  4. Plan your drivers. Each one joins by your invite.
  5. Price the work. Our free Relay load calculator shows a load's net, net per loaded mile and break-even rate from its pay, miles and your costs.

Frequently asked questions

Does Amazon Relay accept new authority?

Amazon says Relay typically requires your DOT number to be at least 180 days old to complete onboarding. It still encourages you to apply early, so it can start reviewing your information.

Can I use Amazon Relay with just a DOT number?

No. Amazon lists a DOT number with interstate authority and a valid MC number. Your entity type also has to be a carrier authorized for property and for hire.

Does leasing on count toward Amazon Relay's 180 days?

Amazon doesn't say. It describes no lease-on route for Relay. The only path it describes under another carrier is that carrier inviting you as a driver.

Is the 180 days counted from my DOT or my MC?

Amazon doesn't say. Its FAQ names the DOT number, its insurance post names the MC, and its box truck post says 6 months. Check your dates with FMCSA and ask Amazon if they differ.

What is an Amazon Relay lease agreement?

Amazon publishes no lease-on agreement for Relay. If you mean renting your trailers to Amazon, that's a different program. See our trailer rental guide.

Can I buy a company to get around the 180 days?

Amazon doesn't address it. Ownership changes go through Amazon at least 30 days ahead, and FMCSA has its own transfer rules. See our business for sale guide.
