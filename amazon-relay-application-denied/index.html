Amazon Relay application denied: what to do next

Got a rejection from Amazon Relay? Here's what Amazon says about reapplying, what it checks and what to fix before you apply again.

NudoIQ · Published October 16, 2026 · Last updated October 16, 2026

Short answer

Amazon says that in many cases you may be eligible to reapply. Start with the reason for your rejection. Fixable issues like insurance, authority status or missing documents can be addressed first. Serious compliance issues may disqualify you.

Can you reapply after Amazon Relay rejects you?

Often, yes. The Relay FAQ says: "In many cases, you may be eligible to reapply." It adds: "We recommend first reviewing the reason for your rejection."

Amazon splits the reasons in two:

KindAmazon's wordsWhat happens
Fixable"like insurance coverage, authority status, or missing documentation""you're welcome to reapply once it's been addressed"
Possibly final"serious compliance issues"They "may disqualify you from submitting a new application"

Amazon doesn't publish a waiting period, a full list of reasons or which ones count as serious.

Seen "rejection policy" or "rejection penalty"? That's about turning down loads you booked, not your application. See cancelling a load on Relay.

Can you appeal an Amazon Relay rejection?

Amazon doesn't describe an appeal for applications.

The one "no appeal" line we found on Amazon's public Relay pages is about safety. For its violation-rate limits, Amazon says "we won't offer an appeal process." Our safety score guide has those limits.

So the path is: find the reason, fix it, then reapply.

What does Amazon check on an application?

Each of these is something you can check yourself before you apply again:

  • Your FMCSA record. "Make sure to match your application details to the FMCSA database, down to the last dash." Amazon says it confirms this "instantly."
  • Authority. A DOT number with interstate authority active at least 180 days, and a valid MC number. See our new authority guide.
  • Entity type. A "Carrier" entity "Authorized for Property and for Hire."
  • Safety rating. "Satisfactory," "None," or "Not Rated."
  • Insurance. Amazon's minimums. Its vendor gets your certificate from your agent, and a flagged policy goes back through your provider "directly to FMCSA." See insurance requirements and workers' comp.
  • Owners. Amazon verifies "the identity of all beneficial owners (anyone with more than 25% ownership)."
  • California. At onboarding Amazon asks you to upload your CARB documents or attest that you don't plan to operate in California. See our CARB guide.

Amazon also screens beyond FMCSA. Its secure freight page says it validates each carrier's "operating authority, ownership, affiliations, and other business credentials." It doesn't say what "affiliations" means.

How do you find out why you were rejected?

  • Start with the reason. The FAQ tells you to review "the reason for your rejection" first.
  • Use Contact Us. It's on the top right of your application page. That's where Amazon's FAQ points applicants.
  • Skip phone numbers you find online. The FAQ says "Amazon Relay doesn't have an official contact phone number."

Our Relay contact guide lists every route Amazon publishes.

What should you fix before you reapply?

  1. Match FMCSA to your application. Your FMCSA contact info should match "down to the dash or comma." Amazon says its onboarding team uses it to reach you.
  2. Check your authority date. Under 180 days? Amazon says "we encourage you to apply early."
  3. Fix insurance at the source. Get coverage up to Amazon's minimums, then have your provider submit it "directly to FMCSA."
  4. Line up every owner. Anyone over 25% goes through identity verification.
  5. Settle California. Upload your CARB documents or attest. Amazon says "You can always update your attestation later."

What if a driver's profile was rejected?

That's the driver check, not your company's application. Amazon says: "Only drivers who successfully complete an identification check can haul Relay loads." Our background check guide covers the license photo, the selfie and the review time.

Should you buy or rent an approved Relay account?

Amazon's email scams post says: "Never transfer or sell your account to someone you don't know." It lists "Unsolicited offers to buy your business or manage your loads" as a scam sign. Our fraud guide covers account sales.

What doesn't Amazon publish about rejections?

  • An appeal process for applications
  • A full list of rejection reasons
  • Which reasons count as "serious compliance issues"
  • A waiting period before you reapply
  • Which documents prove a business address or ownership
  • What "affiliations" means in its screening

Frequently asked questions

Why was my Amazon Relay application rejected?

Amazon says to start with the reason for your rejection. Its FAQ names insurance coverage, authority status and missing documentation as fixable reasons. It doesn't publish a full list.

How long do I have to wait to reapply to Amazon Relay?

Amazon doesn't publish a waiting period. It says you're welcome to reapply once a fixable issue has been addressed.

Can I appeal an Amazon Relay application rejection?

Amazon doesn't describe an appeal process for applications. Fix the cause, ask through Contact Us on your application page, then reapply.

Is there a phone number for Amazon Relay applications?

No. Amazon's FAQ says Relay doesn't have an official contact phone number. It points applicants to Contact Us on the top right of the application page.

Can I reapply under a new DOT number?

Amazon doesn't say. It verifies every beneficial owner with more than 25% ownership and screens ownership and affiliations, so ask Amazon before you try.

What does profile rejected mean on Amazon Relay?

If it's a driver's profile, it's the driver identity check, not your company application. Only drivers who pass that check can haul Relay loads. See our background check guide.
