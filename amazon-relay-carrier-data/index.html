Amazon Relay carrier data 2026: what FMCSA inspection records show

We counted every FMCSA roadside inspection that lists Amazon as the shipper over three years. The result is a public, repeatable look at how many carriers haul Amazon freight, where they are based, what they drive and whether they stay.

By Caleb, NudoIQ · Published October 2, 2026 · Last updated October 2, 2026

Short answer

A NudoIQ analysis of the FMCSA Vehicle Inspection File found 101,444 roadside inspections listing Amazon as the shipper, across 22,866 distinct carriers (USDOT numbers), from 2023-09-24 to 2026-09-22. About 530 new carriers appear each month, and about 70% of carriers hauling Amazon freight run 1-5 power units. Inspections count Amazon freight, not only Amazon Relay loads.

What are the key findings on Amazon carriers?

We counted public FMCSA roadside inspections that list Amazon as the shipper, queried on 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25. An inspection is not a load, so every count here is a floor. Amazon on the bill of lading means Amazon freight, which is not proof the load was booked through Amazon Relay. The main numbers:

  • From 2023-09-24 to 2026-09-22, 101,444 FMCSA roadside inspections listed Amazon as the shipper, across 22,866 distinct carriers (USDOT numbers).
  • About 16,978 carriers had at least one Amazon-shipper inspection from 2025-01-01 to 2026-09-22, and all of them show as active in the FMCSA census.
  • Roughly 530 carriers per month show up on an Amazon-shipper inspection for the first time: 3,167 new carriers from 2026-04-01 to 2026-09-22.
  • Of 321,311 carriers inspected between 2026-03-22 and 2026-09-22, 7,932 (2.47%, about 1 in 40) had at least one inspection listing Amazon as the shipper.
  • Through 2026-09-22, Amazon-shipper inspections in 2026 (29,335) had already reached about 82% of the full 2025 count (35,753).
  • About 70% of carriers with an Amazon-shipper inspection since 2025-01-01 run 1-5 power units.
  • Of carriers whose last Amazon inspection was 0-3 months before 2026-03-22 and who were inspected again in the next six months, 64.5% were again carrying Amazon freight.

How many carriers haul Amazon freight?

At least 22,866 distinct carriers (USDOT numbers) appeared on FMCSA roadside inspections listing Amazon as the shipper between 2023-09-24 and 2026-09-22, across 101,444 inspections. In the shorter window from 2025-01-01 to 2026-09-22, about 16,978 carriers had at least one such inspection, and all of them show as active in the FMCSA census.

These are floors, since most loads are never inspected. The counts also include Amazon's own fleets and contract carriers, not only carriers booking on the Amazon Relay load board.

Of all 321,311 carriers inspected for any reason between 2026-03-22 and 2026-09-22, 7,932 (2.47%, about 1 in 40) had an Amazon-shipper inspection.

How fast are new carriers showing up?

About 530 carriers per month show up on an Amazon-shipper inspection for the first time. Counting carriers whose first Amazon-shipper inspection fell in each window: 558 since 2026-08-25 (about 30 days), 1,584 since 2026-07-01 (about 90 days) and 3,167 since 2026-04-01 (about 180 days), all through 2026-09-22.

"First seen" means the first time a carrier appears on an Amazon-shipper inspection in the three-year file. Some of these carriers may have hauled Amazon freight earlier without being inspected.

Is Amazon freight volume growing?

Yes, by inspection count. Amazon-shipper inspections rose from 29,036 in 2024 to 35,753 in 2025. Through 2026-09-22, the 2026 count was 29,335, already about 82% of the full 2025 total. The number of distinct carriers inspected with Amazon freight followed the same direction: 9,652 in 2024, 11,322 in 2025.

This chart shows Amazon-shipper inspections and distinct carriers by year.

Read 2023 and 2026 as partial years. The 2023 figures (7,320 inspections, 3,681 carriers) start on 2023-09-24, and 2026 (29,335 inspections, 10,117 carriers) runs through 2026-09-22. Inspection counts are an indirect signal of freight volume, not a direct measure of it.

Where are Amazon carriers based?

California leads by a wide margin, with 3,011 carriers that had an Amazon-shipper inspection since 2025-01-01, by physical address. Ohio (1,689) and Illinois (1,589) come next, ahead of Pennsylvania (1,155) and Texas (972). Ohio and Illinois ranking above Texas is something a general carrier list would not show.

This chart shows the top 12 states by number of carriers.

After Texas come Florida (903), Minnesota (709), Washington (653), New Jersey (568), North Carolina (449), Georgia (404) and Michigan (358). State is the carrier's physical address in the FMCSA census, not where the inspection happened.

How big are the carriers that haul Amazon freight?

Most are small. About 70% of carriers with an Amazon-shipper inspection since 2025-01-01 run 1-5 power units. The single largest group is 1-2 power units, with 9,941 carriers. At the other end, 72 carriers run more than 1,000 power units.

This chart shows carriers by fleet size (power units).

The fleet-size query totals 19,355 carriers, so read it as the shape of the distribution rather than an exact headcount. Amazon itself has no fleet size requirement for Relay, per its truck requirements page. For what Relay does require, see Amazon Relay requirements.

What equipment hauls Amazon freight?

Mostly tractor-trailers. On Amazon-shipper inspections with a weight recorded, 87.7% of vehicles were over 54,000 lb (tractor-trailer class), versus 73.4% on all other 2025+ inspections. In the FMCSA census, 43.1% of carriers with Amazon inspections since 2025 report power only (tractors, no trailers), which fits Amazon's statement that most Relay loads are power only.

This chart shows the equipment carriers report owning in the FMCSA census.

Of 16,943 carriers in that census group: power only 43.1%, tractors and trailers 30.3%, box or straight trucks only 14.6%, other or none listed 12.0%. More on hauling Amazon's trailers on our Amazon Relay power only page.

This chart shows vehicle weight class on Amazon-shipper inspections compared with all other inspections.

Box truck class vehicles (19,501-26,000 lb) made up 7.4% of Amazon-shipper inspection rows, against 9.0% on other 2025+ inspections. At the carrier level, 83.9% of carriers inspected with Amazon freight since 2025-01-01 ran tractor-trailers and 13.3% ran box trucks. See Amazon Relay box truck loads for that side of the board.

On the 55,756 units from 2026 Amazon-shipper inspections, 47.9% were truck tractors and 45.9% semi-trailers, with straight trucks at 4.8%. The top tractor makes were Freightliner (15,957), Volvo (6,448) and Kenworth (1,302). The top trailer makes were Hyundai Translead (12,738), Great Dane (3,866), Stoughton (1,922) and Wabash (1,283).

Do carriers keep hauling Amazon freight?

Recent and frequent Amazon carriers mostly keep hauling it. Of carriers last inspected with Amazon freight 0-3 months before 2026-03-22 and inspected again in the next six months, 64.5% were carrying Amazon freight again. For carriers last seen 27-30 months earlier, that dropped to 15.2%. History matters too: 66.5% with 10+ prior Amazon inspections, 24.7% with one.

How was this data collected?

We used the FMCSA Vehicle Inspection File, a public dataset updated daily, plus FMCSA's Inspections Per Unit file and Company Census. We counted inspections whose shipper name contains "AMAZON" and the distinct USDOT numbers behind them. The public file covers 2023-09-24 to 2026-09-22. We publish aggregates only, with no carrier names or DOT numbers.

Sources (queried 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25):

  • FMCSA Vehicle Inspection File, dataset fx4q-ay7w: data.transportation.gov/d/fx4q-ay7w
  • FMCSA Inspections Per Unit, dataset wt8s-2hbx: data.transportation.gov/d/wt8s-2hbx
  • FMCSA Company Census, dataset az4n-8mr2: data.transportation.gov/d/az4n-8mr2

How it works. When an officer does a roadside inspection, the report records the shipper named on the bill of lading. The public file is a rolling three-year window. FMCSA removes driver information from public inspection files.

Caveats:

  • An inspection is not a load. Most loads are never inspected, so these counts are a floor, not a total.
  • Amazon freight is not the same as Amazon Relay. Amazon on the bill of lading means Amazon freight, which is not proof the load was booked through Relay. It includes Amazon's own fleets and contract carriers.
  • Shipper name is often blank. It is filled in on 67.0% of all inspection records (8,314,133 records in the file on 2026-09-24). In 2026, capture by inspection level was 73% for Level 2, 68% for Level 1, 68% for Level 3 and 8% for Level 5 (vehicle only).
  • Spelling varies. Officers spell Amazon 1,064 different ways in the file. Plain "AMAZON" is the most common.

Frequently asked questions

How many trucking companies haul for Amazon?

At least 22,866 carriers (USDOT numbers) appeared on FMCSA roadside inspections listing Amazon as the shipper between 2023-09-24 and 2026-09-22. About 16,978 did from 2025-01-01 to 2026-09-22. Because most loads are never inspected, the real number is higher.

How many carriers are on Amazon Relay?

This analysis can't give a Relay-only count. Public FMCSA inspection data shows carriers hauling Amazon freight in general, which includes Relay carriers, Amazon's own fleets and contract carriers, and it can't separate Relay loads from other Amazon freight.

Which state has the most Amazon carriers?

California, with 3,011 carriers that had an Amazon-shipper inspection since 2025-01-01, by physical address. Ohio (1,689) and Illinois (1,589) are next, both ahead of Texas (972).

Are most Amazon carriers owner-operators?

Most are small. About 70% of carriers with an Amazon-shipper inspection since 2025-01-01 run 1-5 power units, and 9,941 run just 1-2.

Is this data updated?

The FMCSA Vehicle Inspection File is updated daily and covers a rolling three-year window. The figures on this page come from queries run on 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25 and cover inspections through 2026-09-22.
