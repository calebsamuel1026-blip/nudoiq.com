How Much Does Amazon Relay Pay?

Amazon Relay rates vary based on demand, season, time of day, pickup location, delivery destination, equipment type and your booking history. There's no "average rate": each load is priced independently. Here's how Relay pricing works and how to judge whether a rate makes sense for your business.

NudoIQ · Published September 20, 2026 · Last updated October 2, 2026

Short answer

Amazon Relay pay varies by load, and Amazon does not publish typical rates. Each rate includes a base rate, a fuel surcharge and tolls, and moves with distance, deadhead, trip direction, equipment, live loads, season and region. Amazon pays weekly: loads finished by 11:59 pm Saturday are paid the following Friday.

What Sets Amazon Relay Rates

Amazon Relay pricing is dynamic. No two loads are priced identically. Several factors influence the posted rate:

Season Demand for freight changes through the year. Amazon lists seasonal demand as a rate factor and says load volume nearly doubles during peak periods like Prime Day and the holidays.

Time of Day When a load posts and picks up can change its price. Amazon doesn't publish which hours pay more, so watch your own lanes.

Origin and Destination Different lanes have different characteristics. Where you're picking up and delivering affects what Amazon offers, and Amazon says distance, including deadhead and trip direction, is part of the price. Regional market conditions and load characteristics (for example, live loads) matter too.

Equipment Type Amazon lists equipment type and availability as a rate factor, using reefer as an example. It doesn't publish rate comparisons between equipment types.

Your Booking History Your record affects what you see first. Amazon gives A+, A and B+ carriers early access to new spot loads, and cancelling loads you booked lowers your acceptance score.

Daily Volume on the Board How much freight is on the board that day affects what you see and what it pays. Amazon doesn't publish day-of-week or hour-by-hour patterns.

Booking Timing The moment a good load posts, it typically books within seconds. If you're not actively refreshing or using an auto-refresh tool, you'll miss premium loads and see older, less attractive freight. This is why auto-refresh extensions are widespread among Relay carriers.

Why the Same Lane Pays Differently Hour to Hour

Here's a made-up example to show how it can play out:

Tuesday, 6:00 AM: A load posts from Distribution Center A to Hub B. Amazon posts it at a certain rate. It books within seconds.

Tuesday, 11:00 AM: Another load posts: same origin, same destination, same equipment type, same distance. Amazon posts it at a lower rate. This load sits longer because carriers are less interested.

Why the difference? Amazon may have posted the first load with lower freight volume available that morning, so it priced higher to attract carriers. By 11 AM, more freight arrived, so Amazon prices softer. Or more carriers came online, so competition increased. Or Amazon's algorithm expected this lane to need coverage anyway, so it discounted it.

The point: there's no "typical" rate for a lane. Rates change constantly, even for the same route. Each posting is independent.

Weekly Payment Cycle

Amazon Relay pays weekly, regardless of rate:

  • Payment Period: Sunday 12:00 AM through Saturday 11:59 PM
  • Payment Date: The following Friday
  • Invoice Generation: Automatic: no manual invoicing required
  • Pay Disputes: Must be filed within 30 days of the original load completion date

Weekly pay on a set schedule is one of Amazon Relay's consistent advantages.

How to Judge Whether a Rate Is Good

Since rates vary constantly and there's no industry benchmark, how do you know if a posted rate is worth accepting?

Calculate Your True Cost

A posted rate is gross revenue, not profit. To know if a load is worth your time:

  1. Start with the posted rate.
  2. Subtract your estimated fuel cost for the round trip.
  3. Subtract vehicle maintenance (tire wear, oil, parts depreciation).
  4. Subtract deadhead miles to the pickup location (if you're not already positioned there).
  5. What's left is your potential net profit.

The formula is straightforward: Posted rate minus your fuel, maintenance and deadhead costs equals your net profit. The same distance can yield different profits depending on your costs and positioning.

Set a Personal Profit Floor

Many carriers set a minimum net profit they require below which they decline a load. This prevents low-margin work and protects your revenue.

Check Lane History

If you've run a lane before, you know its typical rate range. If a lane is posted below its usual range, it's a soft day for that route. You can decline and wait for a better rate, or accept if you need volume.

If you're new to a lane, you have no history. Lane Intelligence tools (like NudoIQ) track posted rates on every lane over 90 days. They show you the target price, historical range and confidence level. This helps you know if a posted rate is strong or soft compared to history.

Consider the Positioning

If you're already at or near the pickup location, you can accept lower rates because you have zero deadhead. If you're far from the pickup, deadhead costs will eat into your margin and may make the load unprofitable.

Know When to Refresh

Amazon doesn't publish which hours pay best. Track your own lanes by time of day, and use alerts so you see new loads when they post.

Factors That DON'T Affect Your Rate

Your Safety Rating or BASIC Score Amazon doesn't publish any rate premium for a clean safety record. Your safety record decides whether you can haul at all, and your performance grade affects which loads you see first.

Your Equipment Condition or Age New or meticulously maintained equipment doesn't command higher rates. Amazon cares only that it's approved equipment.

Haggling on Spot Loads Spot loads book at the listed price. The ways to set your own price are Post A Truck (a minimum rate per mile), auctions (bids on contracts up to 6 months, most needing a C+ grade) and Relay Assistant (naming a price on eligible urgent loads, for top-performing carriers; Amazon may counter).

Pay Disputes and 30-Day Deadline

If you think Amazon miscalculated a load, charged you a fee, or disputed a completion incorrectly, you have 30 days from the load date to file a claim. File sooner rather than later: pay disputes must be filed within 30 days.

Pay disputes are separate from scorecard disputes. A pay dispute is about money owed. A scorecard dispute is about a violation on your performance record (late delivery, rejected load, etc.).

Understanding Rate Variability

The takeaway: Amazon Relay does not publish "typical" or "average" rates because they vary so much by lane, time, season and carrier profile. Rates on the same route can fluctuate significantly day to day. Don't chase a single good rate you saw yesterday: instead, build a system (auto-refresh, profit floor, lane history) to evaluate each load individually and decide if it's worth your costs.

Many carriers use auto-refresh tools or auto-booking extensions to remove emotion and speed from the decision. Frequently asked questions

How much money can I make on Amazon Relay?

There's no typical earning amount because rates vary so much by lane, season, time of day and your costs. Calculate net profit load-by-load and decide if each one meets your minimum. Some days you'll see good rates; other days you won't.

Are rates higher in the morning or evening?

Amazon doesn't publish which hours pay more. Rates move with demand, so the same lane can pay differently at different times. Track your own lanes to see the pattern.

Can I negotiate a rate with Amazon?

Not on most spot loads, which book at the listed price. On select urgent loads, top-performing carriers can name a price through Relay Assistant, and Amazon may approve or counter. Post A Truck lets you set a minimum rate per mile, and auctions let you bid on contracts.

Do owner-operators make more than small fleet carriers?

Amazon doesn't publish anything that ties pay to fleet size, and there's no fleet size requirement to haul for Relay. Your performance grade decides access to things like contracts and early spot loads, and auction eligibility also considers equipment count, tenure and CARB compliance.

How long does it take to get paid after I complete a load?

If you complete the load before 11:59 PM Saturday, you're paid the following Friday. If you finish it after midnight, it falls into the next Sunday to Saturday cycle and is paid the Friday after that cycle ends. So a load finished late Saturday is paid six days later, and one finished early Sunday is paid 12 days later.

What if I dispute a load's rate?

You have 30 days from the load completion date to file a pay dispute. Contact Amazon Relay support through the portal and explain why you think the amount was wrong. Amazon reviews it and decides.
