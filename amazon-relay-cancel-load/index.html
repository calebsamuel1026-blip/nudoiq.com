What happens if you cancel a load on Amazon Relay?

Every carrier ends up with a load they can't run. Here's how Amazon scores a cancellation, why timing matters so much, what a lower grade costs you, and the parts Amazon doesn't publish.

NudoIQ · Published October 8, 2026 · Last updated October 8, 2026

Short answer

Cancelling (rejecting) a load you booked on Amazon Relay counts against your acceptance metric, and so does letting a booked load expire. Amazon says the closer to pickup you reject, the higher the penalty. Your overall score equals your lowest metric, so a weak acceptance score can lower your grade. Loads that Amazon cancels don't count against you.

What counts as a cancellation on Amazon Relay?

Amazon calls it a rejection. Its performance score guide defines the acceptance metric as "How often a carrier rejects loads that they book." Amazon's grades page says the same thing in other words: booking and completing loads without rejections, meaning carrier cancellations.

Two things count:

  • Rejecting a load you booked. Any time you give a tendered load back, your score can take a hit.
  • Letting a booked load expire. Amazon says this counts too. Doing nothing isn't a way out.

Amazon is plain about who owns a booking: "if an Amazon load is booked, it's the Relay carrier's responsibility to execute it."

Does it matter when you cancel?

Yes, a lot. Amazon says: "The closer a carrier rejects to pick-up time, the higher the penalty."

The practical rule is simple. The moment you know a load won't work, release it. A cancellation two days out costs less than the same cancellation two hours out. Amazon doesn't publish how much less, but it does say the penalty rises as pickup gets closer.

What if Amazon cancels the load?

Then it doesn't count against you. Amazon says that if it cancels tendered loads, the carrier's performance score won't be impacted.

You also don't lose the time completely. Amazon's load board guide says the board suggests replacements for cancelled loads. Check the pickup window, deadhead and your driver's hours before you take one.

How does one cancellation affect your whole grade?

Your overall score equals your lowest metric. Amazon scores four: on-time, acceptance, app usage and disruption-free. If acceptance is your weakest, it sets your grade, even with a perfect on-time record.

You become eligible for a grade after ten or more Relay loads in the last six active weeks. Our scorecard guide breaks down all four metrics.

What does a lower grade cost you?

Access. Here's what Amazon says each grade level gets you:

All gradesLoad board access, Relay Rewards eligibility, Post A Truck (higher grades get priority matching)
C+ and upMost freight auctions (B for reefer, B+ for some box truck loads)
B and upSeeing and booking new contracts
A+, A and B+Early access to new spot loads
A+ and AContract price alerts
Below C+Contracts you already booked are at risk

Relay Rewards uses your lowest score too. Amazon evaluates your tier monthly, using your company's lowest score from the previous month. So a bad stretch on acceptance can reach past this week's loads into contracts, auctions and rewards. Our guides to Amazon Relay contracts and auctions explain what's on the line in each.

How do you avoid having to cancel?

Amazon's own advice is to be selective when you browse the load board, so you don't book loads you can't haul. In practice:

  1. Do the hours math before you book. First load, deadhead and second load against your driver's remaining hours. Our guide to booking multiple loads has a checklist.
  2. Check the pickup window, not just the price. Can your driver make it with room to spare?
  3. Use linked orders in Post A Truck. If one truck is posted on several orders, linking them means unfulfilled trips are cancelled for you once one fills. See how to post a truck on Amazon Relay.
  4. Keep your Post A Truck availability current. Cancel an order the moment plans change, before it matches a load.
  5. Release early when you must. The penalty rises as pickup gets closer.

What doesn't Amazon publish about cancellations?

More than most carriers expect. Amazon doesn't publish:

  • Cancellation fees. The Relay FAQ says nothing about cancellation fees, or about pay when a truck order isn't used.
  • The size of the penalty for a single rejection.
  • How long one cancellation stays on your score. Amazon says the score reflects your last six weeks of hauls.
  • The score cutoffs for each letter grade.
  • A dispute route for acceptance. Amazon's dispute language on the performance score page covers on-time impacts only. It doesn't describe a way to dispute an acceptance hit.

If someone quotes you exact numbers for any of these, ask where they got them.

Frequently asked questions

Does rejecting a load on Amazon Relay hurt your score?

Yes. Rejecting a load you booked counts against your acceptance metric, and the closer to pickup you reject, the higher the penalty. Because your overall score is your lowest metric, acceptance can set your whole grade.

Is there a fee for cancelling an Amazon Relay load?

Amazon doesn't publish one. The Relay FAQ says nothing about cancellation fees. The cost Amazon does describe is to your performance score.

What happens if Amazon cancels my Relay load?

Your score isn't affected. Amazon says carrier scores aren't impacted when it cancels tendered loads, and the load board suggests replacements for cancelled loads.

Does letting a load expire count as a rejection on Amazon Relay?

Yes. Amazon says a performance score can take a hit any time a carrier rejects tendered loads, including letting loads expire.

Can I dispute an acceptance hit on my Relay scorecard?

Amazon doesn't publish a dispute route for acceptance. Its dispute language covers on-time impacts. For account-access problems, Amazon's FAQ points to the Support Center when you're logged in. The 24/7 Relay Operations Center handles operational support through the Relay portal or Relay mobile.

How long does a cancellation stay on my Amazon Relay score?

Amazon doesn't say how long a single cancellation stays. It says your score reflects your last six weeks of hauls.
