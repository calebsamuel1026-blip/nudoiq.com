Amazon Relay box truck loads: how to find and book them

Amazon Relay has work for 16 ft and 26 ft box trucks with a liftgate. Here's where those loads show up, how to filter for them, and how to tell a good one from a bad one. You need an approved Relay carrier account to book any of it.

NudoIQ · Published October 2, 2026 · Last updated October 2, 2026

Short answer

Amazon Relay has box truck loads for 16 ft and 26 ft dock-height box trucks with a working tuck-under liftgate. Carriers find them by filtering the Relay load board by equipment type, posting the truck with Post A Truck, or booking box truck contracts, including 8-hour and 13-hour solo contracts with round trips.

What box truck loads does Amazon Relay have?

Amazon Relay has work for 16 ft and 26 ft box trucks that are dock height and have a working tuck-under liftgate. Box trucks can book one-way loads, spot loads from the board and short-term contracts, including 8-hour and 13-hour solo contracts with round trips. Cargo vans can't be used.

That's from Amazon's truck requirements page. To check your truck, insurance and authority first, see our box truck requirements guide.

  • One-way loads. Amazon says one-way loads need a box truck or your own trailer, an edge over a tractor with no trailer.
  • Spot freight. Last-minute loads you book right away.
  • Contracts. Full or partial weeks of loads booked ahead. A 13-hour solo contract is one day of loads with 11 hours of drive time.
  • Post A Truck. You post when and where the truck is free, and Amazon matches loads to it.

If you're looking for a 26 ft box truck load board for Amazon freight, the Relay board is it. Amazon tenders its loads only through Relay, not on third-party boards.

How do you filter the Relay load board for box truck loads?

Open the Relay load board and set the equipment type filter to the box truck size you run. Then narrow by origin or radius, load type, driver type, trip length and pickup window, and save the search. Amazon says the board is open 24/7 and free to search and book.

A setup that works for most box truck carriers:

  1. Equipment type: your box truck size only, so you don't scroll past loads you can't run.
  2. Origin and radius: where the truck sits and how far you'll drive empty.
  3. Load type and driver type: one-way, round trip or both; solo unless you run a team.
  4. Max stops and trip length: what one shift can handle.
  5. Rate range and time windows: cut what you'd never book.
  6. Save the search so it's there every time.

The board also suggests return trips and reloads after you book. If it looks empty, work through our load board not showing loads checklist. The Amazon Relay load board guide covers the rest.

How do you set up Post A Truck for a box truck?

Post A Truck lets you post your box truck's availability with a minimum rate per mile, and Amazon matches loads to it. Amazon lists box trucks as eligible, along with power only and live loads. Carriers who get matched see that freight first, before it ever reaches the load board.

From Amazon's Post A Truck guide, here's what you set:

  • Window: post the truck and driver up to two weeks ahead.
  • Direction: one-way or round trip.
  • Cities: include the ones you want, exclude the ones you don't.
  • Limits: a cap on distance or duration.
  • Price: a minimum rate per mile.

Before you post, read the Match outlook: High, Medium or Low odds based on the previous six weeks of loads. If it's Low, widen the cities or the hours.

A block order (same start and end) pays in full even if Amazon doesn't use the whole block. Amazon books loads that match your criteria, so only post a window you can run.

Should a box truck run Relay contracts or spot loads?

Spot loads are last-minute loads you book on the board right away. Contracts are full or partial weeks of loads booked ahead. Contract access depends on your grade: Amazon says new contracts need a B or better, and a grade needs ten or more Relay loads in the last six active weeks.

How common are box trucks on Amazon freight?

Box trucks are a real but small share of Amazon freight. In our count of FMCSA roadside inspections that list Amazon as the shipper, the box truck weight class (19,501 to 26,000 lb) is 7.4% of inspection rows. At the carrier level, 13.3% of those carriers were inspected in a box truck.

More from the same public FMCSA data, which we break down on our Amazon Relay carrier data page:

  • 14.6% of carriers with Amazon-shipper inspections since 2025 list only box or straight trucks in the FMCSA census.
  • Straight trucks were 4.8% of units on 2026 Amazon-shipper inspections. The top makes were Freightliner, International and Hino.

Two caveats. An inspection isn't a load: most loads are never inspected, so these counts are a floor, not a total. And Amazon on the bill of lading means Amazon freight, not proof the load was booked through Relay; it includes Amazon's own fleets and contract carriers.

How do you tell if a box truck rate is good?

Start with what the price includes. Amazon says a Relay rate shows a base rate, a fuel surcharge that tracks fuel prices, and tolls. Then take out your own costs and the empty miles to pickup. Distance, trip direction, equipment, live loads, season and region all move the rate.

Ask on every load:

  • How far is the deadhead? You pay for the empty miles to pickup.
  • Which way does it leave you? A one-way that ends near home beats one that strands the truck two hours out.
  • Is it a live load? Check the details so the wait doesn't eat the shift.
  • What's left after costs? Fuel, maintenance and driver pay come out first.

Pay varies by lane, timing and equipment, and Amazon doesn't publish typical box truck rates. More on that in how much Amazon Relay pays.

Why do box truck loads disappear so fast?

Good loads on the Relay board get booked within seconds because a lot of carriers watch the same filters. Amazon also shows new spot loads to A+, A and B+ carriers first, at staggered times. By the time you reload by hand, a clean round trip near you can already be gone.

What helps:

  • Amazon's own alerts. Saved searches, email alerts and mobile push notifications.
  • A refresher. It reloads the board for you on a timer. See how Amazon Relay auto refresh works.
  • Rules and auto-booking. An auto-booker books only loads that match your lane, price and limits. See our auto-booker guide.
  • Protecting acceptance. Cancelling a booked load hurts your acceptance score, more the closer it is to pickup. Book only what the truck can run.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use a cargo van for Amazon Relay loads?

No. Amazon says cargo vans can't be used on Relay. Its box truck options are 16 ft and 26 ft trucks with a working tuck-under liftgate.

Do I need a liftgate for Amazon box truck loads?

Yes. Amazon requires a working tuck-under liftgate on both 16 ft and 26 ft box trucks. A box truck without one doesn't meet the equipment rules.

Is there a minimum fleet size to haul box truck loads for Amazon?

No. Amazon says there's no fleet size requirement to haul for Relay, so a single box truck can qualify. Your authority, insurance and safety record still have to meet the Amazon Relay requirements.

Are Amazon box truck loads posted on DAT or other load boards?

No. Amazon tenders its loads only through the Relay load board, not on third-party boards. You need an approved Relay carrier account to see and book them.

How fast does Amazon pay for box truck loads?

Amazon pays weekly on a Sunday to Saturday cycle. Loads finished before 11:59 pm Saturday are paid the following Friday, and invoices are created automatically, per the Relay FAQ.
