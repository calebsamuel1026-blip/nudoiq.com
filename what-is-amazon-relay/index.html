What Is Amazon Relay?

Amazon Relay is Amazon's load board platform where carriers book freight loads posted by Amazon. It's a centralized place to find work, manage dispatches and track your compliance with Amazon's performance metrics.

NudoIQ · Published September 20, 2026 · Last updated October 2, 2026

Short answer

Amazon Relay is Amazon's platform for carriers to find and book Amazon freight. Approved carriers with a DOT number active at least 180 days and a valid MC number book spot loads, round trips, blocks and short-term contracts, or post availability with Post A Truck. Amazon pays weekly and grades carriers on a performance score.

Overview

Amazon Relay is an on-demand load board built directly into Amazon's fulfillment network. Unlike traditional freight boards where shippers post lanes and carriers bid, Amazon Relay is where Amazon posts its own work: one-way loads, round trips, blocks and short-term contracts. Loads are booked first-come, first-served through the load board, meaning that fast refreshing and split-second booking decisions determine who gets the best freight.

Amazon says there's no fleet size requirement, so owner-operators and small fleets can use it. You sign in with your business credentials, and Amazon tenders its loads only through Relay. Spot loads show an all-in price you can book as listed. You can also post your truck with the Post A Truck feature, set a minimum rate per mile, and get matched to work.

Who Can Join Amazon Relay

Amazon has specific eligibility requirements to protect load security and ensure compliance:

  • Your DOT number must have interstate authority and be active for at least 180 days (a newer authority may disqualify you).
  • You must have a valid MC (Motor Carrier) number.
  • Your FMCSA safety rating must be Satisfactory, None, or Not Rated. A rating below Satisfactory disqualifies you.
  • Your BASIC scores must stay within limits: Unsafe Driving below 60%, HOS Compliance below 60%, Vehicle Maintenance below 75%, Controlled Substances/Alcohol below 75%, Driver Fitness below 75%. Your driver out-of-service violation rate must be at or below 35%, and vehicle violation rate at or below 50%.
  • Amazon verifies your business entity and every beneficial owner, meaning anyone with more than 25% ownership in your carrier.

The 180-day authority rule is the main gate for new carriers. Amazon's FAQ says rejected applicants may be able to reapply once a fixable issue (such as authority status) is resolved.

What Equipment Works on Relay

Relay supports specific equipment types, and what you own affects your load volume and rates:

  • Day cab and sleeper tractors (most loads available)
  • 26 ft and 16 ft box trucks with a working tuck-under liftgate (the liftgate is required)
  • 48 ft and 53 ft dry van and reefer trailers
  • 20, 40 and 45 ft high-cube containers (UIIA compliant)

Cargo vans are not supported: this is an explicit exclusion. Even if you think a large cargo van could handle loads, Amazon does not accept it on the platform.

Amazon says the majority of Relay loads are power only: you bring the tractor and Amazon provides the trailer. Round trips usually need only a tractor, while one-way loads need a box truck or your own trailer.

How Loads Get Posted and Booked

Amazon tenders loads only through the Relay load board: not on third-party boards, DATs, or by email. When you log in to Relay, you see every available load that matches your equipment type in real time.

There are four main ways to find work on Relay:

The Load Board

Browse one-way and round-trip loads instantly. You see the posted rate, origin, destination, and estimated pick-up time before you commit. Book immediately if it fits your route and profit requirements. Good loads book within seconds, which is why auto-refresh tools and browser extensions exist.

Blocks

A chunk of time you reserve trucks and drivers for Relay. Amazon pays for the block even if it doesn't use the full block.

Short-Term Contracts

Short-term contracts cover full or partial weeks of loads, booked in advance. New contracts need a B grade or better, and freight auctions let carriers bid on contracts of up to 6 months.

Post A Truck

Instead of hunting the board, you post your truck's availability up to two weeks ahead with a minimum rate per mile, and Amazon matches you to loads. This reduces the need for constant refreshing and is helpful for carriers who want steadier work.

Understanding the Performance Scorecard

Amazon grades carriers on four metrics, based on the last six weeks of hauls:

  • On-time: Loads arriving by the planned time at origin and destination.
  • Acceptance: How often you cancel loads you booked, including expired loads. The closer to pickup, the bigger the penalty.
  • App usage: Drivers keeping the Relay app running with location on for the whole trip.
  • Disruption-free: No stationary trailers and no loads without an assigned driver.

Your overall score is the lowest of the four, shown as a letter grade from F to A+. The grade affects which work you can book, such as new contracts and early access to new spot loads. If something happens in transit, you can dispute a score impact in your Relay account, and Amazon makes the final decision. See our scorecard guide for details.

Pay Cycle and Rate Variability

Amazon pays weekly. Your pay cycle runs Sunday to Saturday. Any load you complete before 11:59 pm Saturday gets paid the following Friday. Invoices are generated automatically, so there's no invoice chase needed.

Load rates on Amazon Relay vary based on multiple factors: season, time of day, pickup origin, delivery destination, your equipment type, your own booking history and how many other carriers are online that day. The same lane can pay very differently on different days, depending on Amazon's demand and your carrier profile. This is why learning to read a lane's rate history is important: it gives you context for whether a posted rate is good or soft.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sign in to Amazon Relay?

Visit relay.amazon.com and sign in with your business credentials. You'll complete Amazon's approval process, which includes verifying your DOT number, MC number, insurance, safety rating and beneficial ownership. Amazon doesn't publish an approval timeline.

Can I run Amazon Relay part-time?

Yes. You set your own availability with Post A Truck, and you choose which loads to book on the board. Amazon says there's no fleet size requirement. Just remember that cancelling loads you booked hurts your acceptance score.

What happens if I miss a pickup?

A late or missed pickup counts against your on-time metric, and a lower grade can reduce your load access. You can file a dispute if the miss was beyond your control (weather, breakdown, traffic) but Amazon makes the final decision.

Does my scorecard affect how much I get paid?

Amazon doesn't say your grade changes a load's price. It changes access: A+, A and B+ carriers see new spot loads first, B and above can book new contracts, and higher grades get priority matching on Post A Truck.

Is there a minimum rate or maximum distance?

Amazon doesn't publish a minimum rate. You decide whether a posted rate justifies your cost to run it, and with Post A Truck you can set your own minimum rate per mile. Many carriers set their own profit floor and reject anything below: NudoIQ lets you set that rule and filter loads automatically.

Can I use a cargo van?

No. Amazon Relay does not accept cargo vans. Only box trucks 16 ft and 26 ft with a working tuck-under liftgate, tractors with trailers, or supported containers.
