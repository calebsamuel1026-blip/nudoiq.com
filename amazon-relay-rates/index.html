Amazon Relay rates by lane

One-way Amazon Relay loads had a median of $2.90 per mile on the board, and round trips $2.36. Most of the 4,066 loads (84%) started in Tennessee, so this is not a national average.

NudoIQ data · Loads first seen July 7, 2026 to October 5, 2026 · Published October 5, 2026 · Last updated October 5, 2026

Short answer

NudoIQ users saw 4,066 distinct Amazon Relay loads first seen from July 7 to October 5, 2026. One-way loads had a median final board price of $420 and a median of $2.90 per mile (median trip 161 miles; middle half $2.35 to $3.38 per mile). Round trips had a median of $2.36 per mile, because their miles include the drive back. 57% of loads were raised in price at least once while posted. Most loads started in Tennessee, where NudoIQ users search most.

Method

  • Source. Amazon Relay loads that appeared on the load board in the browsers of NudoIQ users, recorded by the NudoIQ extension: price, miles, origin, destination and the time each load was seen. Amazon did not provide, review or endorse this data, and NudoIQ is not affiliated with Amazon.
  • Window. Loads first seen from July 7, 2026 to October 5, 2026 (90 days, Eastern Time). Data exists for 47 of those days.
  • One load, one row. The extension can log the same load many times (every tab, every refresh, a check every 10 minutes). Each load is counted once by its load ID.
  • Price. “Final price” is the last price a NudoIQ user saw for the load before it left the board. Relay prices go up and down while a load is posted, so the first price seen is reported separately. These are board prices, not what any carrier was paid or earned, and they do not include your costs.
  • Rate per mile. Final price divided by the miles shown on the board. Round trips are split from one-way loads because their miles include the drive back. A load is a round trip when Relay marks it as one, when it ends where it started, or when its miles are at least 1.7 times the shortest trip seen between the same two cities. Loads that could not be classified count in “All loads” only.
  • Medians and middle 50%. Medians, with the 25th to 75th percentile range, so a few unusual loads do not move the numbers.
  • Lanes. A lane is an origin state and destination state. A lane gets a page when it has at least 30 distinct loads seen on at least 10 different days. City pairs are shown with at least 30 loads, and a trip-type figure with at least 15 loads.
  • Time on the board. Minutes from the first time a NudoIQ user saw a load to the last time, counted only for loads that were missing when the same lane was checked again 11 to 40 minutes later. A load may have been posted before anyone saw it, and leaving the board does not always mean it was booked, so treat this as a guide. At least 30 such loads are needed to show the figure.
  • Time of day. Tested on one-way loads only, after allowing for trip length (shorter runs price higher per mile). A difference is shown only when p < 0.005 and the gap is at least 5%. “When first seen” is when a NudoIQ user first saw the load, not when Amazon posted it.
  • Limits. The data follows where NudoIQ users search, so most loads start in Tennessee, and the lanes here are not a national sample. Equipment type is not recorded. Numbers will change as more loads are seen.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average Amazon Relay rate per mile?

The median Amazon Relay rate in this data was $2.90 per mile for one-way loads and $2.36 for round trips. That is from 4,066 loads NudoIQ users saw from July 7 to October 5, 2026. Half of the one-way loads fell between $2.35 and $3.38 per mile. Most of the loads (84%) started in Tennessee, so this mostly reflects Tennessee lanes.

Do Amazon Relay round trips pay less per mile than one-way loads?

Yes, round trips had a median of $2.36 per mile, compared with $2.90 for one-way loads. On the same city pair, round trips were priced at a median 0.68 times the one-way rate per mile (41 city pairs). Round-trip miles include the drive back, and in dollars the median round trip was higher, $575 against $420.

How fast do loads leave the Amazon Relay load board?

The median Amazon Relay load stayed on the board 1.8 hours after a NudoIQ user first saw it (1,807 loads seen leaving). Of those, 37% were gone within 30 minutes, but 42% were still posted after 4 hours. We can see that a load left the board, not who booked it or why. Some loads were also posted before they were first seen.

Does Amazon raise the price of Relay loads that are not booked?

Yes, on 57% of loads the board price went up at least once while they were posted. Just over half (54%) ended higher than the first price seen, with a median rise of 16.9%. Prices were also cut at least once on 29% of loads. The median first price seen was $406, against a median final price of $443.

Are these Amazon Relay rates from Amazon?

No, they come from 4,066 loads NudoIQ users saw on the Amazon Relay board from July 7 to October 5, 2026. Amazon did not provide, review or endorse this data, and NudoIQ is not affiliated with Amazon. The numbers are observed board prices, not earnings. Other carriers may see different loads and prices on their own board.

Why are most of these lanes in Tennessee?

Most of the loads in this data (84%) started in Tennessee, because that is where NudoIQ users search. Maryville, TN alone accounts for 2,725 of the 4,066 loads. A lane gets its own page once it has at least 30 loads seen on at least 10 different days in 90 days. More lanes will be added as users in other areas search.
